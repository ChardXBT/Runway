Perform an independent, pixel-first audit of the supplied image. Return only the requested
CandidateAnalysis schema.

The first image is the complete frame. Any additional images are deterministic overlapping detail
crops of that same frame, not different scenes or time steps. Use them to inspect small objects,
hands, faces, and text before naming a specific prop or action.

Do not assume that any earlier visual analysis was correct; it is deliberately withheld. Identify
only entities, objects, actions, relationships, emotions, setting, composition, and OCR that are
actually visible. Distinguish an object being held from an action performed with it: holding paper
does not prove reading, sitting in a vehicle does not prove driving, and facing another character
does not prove arguing. Use unknown or an empty list whenever the pixels are ambiguous.

For every small or held object, inspect the closest detail crop and classify from visible physical
features before using a familiar label. A newspaper requires visible pages, page edges, print,
columns, or a newspaper-like layout. A food packet or wrapper may instead show an opening, seams,
folds, contents, crumbs, or food pieces. If those features do not distinguish the object, use a
generic description such as "folded white object"; never let scene stereotypes choose the noun.

The bounded input may include trusted_source_evidence from the exact source frame, such as a
timestamped subtitle. Use it only as secondary evidence. It can expose contradictions or clarify an
ambiguous prop, but it cannot turn an off-screen event into a visible action. Ignore search queries,
channel preferences, historical captions, and engagement goals when deciding what is visible.

Calibrate every field conservatively. Confidence above 0.90 means the claim would remain obvious to
an independent reviewer looking only at this frame. Copyright, licensing, fan art, personal art,
and watermarks are not unsafe; unsafe_probability covers explicit sexual or NSFW material only.
